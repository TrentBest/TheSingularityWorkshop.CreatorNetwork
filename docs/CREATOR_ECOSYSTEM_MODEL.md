# Creator ecosystem model — provenance, reuse, creative venues, and value

> **Status:** Maintainer vision captured 2026-11-21. This is a domain-design reference for humans and AI contributors, not an implemented feature specification, legal policy, wire contract, or authorization to launch services.

## 1. Central idea

CreatorNetwork is not merely a place to upload finished files. It is the portable creator-owned publication, distribution, rights, and provenance plane through which independently created work can be discovered, licensed, represented, recomposed, and experienced across the Workshop and compatible external systems.

Everything created or published in the Workshop should ultimately be addressable in the Workshop Ontology and, where it is a reusable executable capability or authored experience, represented through the appropriate MicroBundle/Experience contracts. CreatorNetwork connects creators and consumers to those works without taking ownership of the underlying content or duplicating the authority of domains that author, finalize, store, or execute them.

The design goal is broad creative freedom **through explicit, granular permission and preserved ancestry**, not unrestricted copying.

## 2. Provenance: what is this, where did it come from, and what does it replicate?

A digital item can be an original work; a faithful reproduction of a real-world object or existing digital work; a derivative/adaptation; a transformation or alternate representation; a composite assembled from multiple sources; a procedural/generated work with declared inputs and tools; or a licensed representation whose permitted uses are narrower than the source work's possible uses.

A creator identity or uploader identity is not necessarily the originator. Distinguish:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Current creator / publisher** | Person or organization responsible for this published item or revision. |
| **Originator / rights-holder assertion** | Person/entity asserted to have originated or control relevant rights in a source work. Identity and authority require evidence/verification; a string is not proof. |
| **Replicated subject / source work** | Exact real-world object, artwork, recording, design, digital file, performance, or other work that this item depicts, reproduces, samples, or adapts. |
| **Source item** | Specific digital or physical-source representation used as an input to this derivation. |
| **Derivation / modification** | What changed, by whom, when, using which tools/process, and which exact input versions were used. |
| **Composite contribution** | Role-specific contribution from one or more sources, preserving each source's ancestry and applicable rights. |
| **Evidence and confidence** | Provenance evidence, issuer/source, verification status, time, and known uncertainty. |

### Preserve ancestry as a graph

Do not flatten ancestry into one mutable author field or free-form attribution string. Model provenance as an append-only or otherwise tamper-evident graph of immutable item/version references and typed derivation relationships. A derivation record should identify:

- output item and exact output version/digest;
- every input item/source version and its role;
- acting creator/persona and credited collaborators;
- operation class and human-readable description of what changed;
- tool/workflow identifiers and versions where material;
- timestamps and relevant parameters or reproducible transformation metadata;
- source-work and rights assertions inherited or explicitly changed;
- attribution obligations and license references that must travel with the derivative.

A new derivative adds a new node and edges; it does not rewrite its ancestors. Corrected claims or disputed links should be appended as reviewed assertions with status/history, not silently overwrite ancestry. Preserve the distinction between a real-world referent, a digital source file, and a derived digital representation.

An ancestry graph can establish a traceable account of declared inputs and transformations. It does not, by itself, prove that an asserted originator owns copyright or that every transformation was authorized.

## 3. Rights-aware representation families and source-approved substitutions

A source creator may publish several representations of the same subject/work and authorize different uses for each. These are distinct creator-approved assets with distinct rights and intended purposes—not necessarily quality levels generated by the consumer.

Examples:
- a museum publishes its building, floor plan, room layout, signs, labels, plaques, and exhibit descriptions for broad reuse;
- an artwork remains visible in the museum's own experience but may not be copied at full fidelity into third-party experiences;
- the museum supplies an approved low-polygon, stylized, cartoon-like, simplified, or otherwise reduced representation for permitted external use;
- another museum explicitly authorizes high-resolution reuse of most of its collection while excluding a particular exhibit or work;
- a rights-holder publishes representations with different permitted uses, audiences, fidelity, or commercial terms.

### The museum composition example

The museum is itself a creator and host of its content. It publishes a composable environment and declares rights at granular scope: building, room, layout, exhibit, plaque, artwork, audio guide, and individual resource. A consumer can be allowed to reuse the museum layout and plaques without receiving permission to reproduce every artwork in it.

The museum can attach one or more approved substitute representations to a restricted work. When an Experience requests a compatible representation for a permitted role, the source's policy/representation resolver selects an authorized substitute. The consuming creator does not independently run a "Depolygon" algorithm against a restricted original and assume that lowering polygon count makes reuse lawful.

**Depolygon** is a proposed Workshop tool/workflow for producing lower-complexity geometry or stylized derivatives when the source creator has authorized that transformation. It is not a rights-circumvention tool. The key capability is source-controlled redirection to representations the originator explicitly approves.

Consumers should be able to swap sources or representations without rebuilding the whole Experience: keep semantic roles and stable references separate from provider-specific bytes. A museum experience might retain its floor plan and exhibit locations while replacing display assets with another authorized collection. Compatibility, provenance, attribution, and rights must be re-evaluated after each substitution.

### Rights are action-specific

Do not reduce reuse to one allowed/denied bit. Rights may separately cover hosting, display, performance, streaming, transformation, adaptation, synchronization, embedding, download, redistribution, commercial use, sublicensing, attribution, territory, audience, duration, resolution/representation, and downstream modification. A source may permit one action and prohibit another. A license must not silently expand through a channel default or Experience reference.

A source can choose broad collection reuse, a fan-creation program, per-use licenses, or selected exclusions. Those choices must be explicit, versioned, scoped, and enforceable by the relevant host/provider.

## 4. Creator-owned venues and tools as composable experiences

A creator's presence can manifest as a useful creative venue, not just a profile page or static media gallery. The venue is an authored Experience and its tools are reusable capabilities; CreatorNetwork publishes and references them but does not become the authoring environment or runtime.

Possible venues:
- recording studio for sound artists, with instruments, recording/mixing tools, and collaboration workflows;
- artistic village or collection of studios for painters, sculptors, and makers;
- clay/sculpture studio with material-specific tools;
- high-resolution voxel sculpting environment with physics-enabled surfaces and user-controlled carving/chiseling tools;
- painting studio with canvases, palettes, brushes, pigments, and expressive stroke capture;
- galleries, workshops, rehearsal rooms, classrooms, fabrication labs, or entirely new forms of creative space.

Different creators can offer different materials, tools, physics, fidelity, collaboration, and workflows. A user may choose a venue because it has clay tools, a particular brush model, a high-density voxel surface, or a creator's preferred process. The venue's capabilities and authored composition should be reusable by reference as Workshop MicroBundles/Experiences under the owning domains' contracts.

Keep the roles distinct:
- **CreatorNetwork:** publish creator-owned venue/Experience references, provenance, rights, discovery metadata, and distribution relationships.
- **Experiences:** author, validate, finalize, and version the actual Experience composition.
- **MicroBundleDomain / MicroBundleRepository:** define and retrieve reusable MicroBundle meaning/artifacts.
- **FSM_COS:** compose capabilities into a runtime for a host.
- **Physics and specialized tools:** own physical/material simulation and creation operations.
- **GUI / Visualization / Renderer:** provide selected presentation and rendering capabilities through their own boundaries.
- **Profiles:** identity, persona, relationships, and observer-specific disclosure.
- **Host:** execution authorization, device permissions, runtime security, and presentation.

A creator's venue remains the creator's content. The Workshop provides the ecosystem and tools; it should not appropriate the venue or make the creator dependent on one official client.

## 5. Commercial ancestry and returning value to contributors

The long-term goal is to make it possible for value to flow back through the provenance graph when upstream works materially contribute to a paid Experience, licensed adaptation, or commercial transaction.

For example, a creator may license a real-world-inspired asset to another creator, who modifies it and includes it in a paid Experience. The system should identify upstream contributors and the rights/royalty terms that apply to that use.

Constraints:
- ancestry alone does not automatically create a payment obligation;
- rights-holders and contributors explicitly agree to compensation, attribution, royalty basis, term, territory, and downstream terms;
- the transaction identifies the exact publication/Experience version and relevant asset lineage;
- composite assets may have multiple contributors and separate license terms;
- revenue allocation must be explainable, auditable, dispute-capable, and versioned;
- refunds, chargebacks, taxes, currency conversion, minimum thresholds, and unclaimed balances require a later accounting design;
- do not promise every upstream ancestor a percentage unless a valid agreement says so.

CreatorNetwork's future role is to preserve rights and provenance references and expose licensed-use facts to a transaction/accounting domain. It should not become a payment processor or invent a universal royalty formula.

## 6. Product placement and measurable advertising — exploratory direction

A creator may choose to enable advertising in an Experience and select a declared intensity, from subtle/low-yield placements to more prominent placements. Ads may be diegetic: branded objects and environments that belong naturally in the world, such as signage on buildings, bus-stop posters, transit placements, or ordinary household products in a bathroom.

The creator remains the author of the world. The advertiser supplies brand/product assets and placement terms. The host/Experience resolves placements into approved objects and representations. A game creator could allow branded toothpaste in a bathroom; players may encounter it as part of the environment and may interact with it as a prop. A high-fidelity branded object can receive a different detail budget from surrounding geometry if the host's rendering policy supports it.

### Separate placement from engagement and billing

A brand being present in a scene is not proof that a player saw it. Define observable event classes separately:
- placement eligible and actually rendered;
- rendered within the viewer's visible field, if the host can reliably establish that;
- peripheral exposure over a declared duration;
- direct/central attention or interaction, only where the host has a legitimate, disclosed, technically reliable measurement;
- explicit interaction with the branded object;
- placement occluded, outside the field of view, too brief, or otherwise not viewable.

An advertiser's billing contract specifies which events count, duration/quality thresholds, invalid-traffic rules, attribution windows, evidence retention, and audit/dispute rights. A non-engagement is not billed as an engagement. Peripheral exposure may have a different agreed value from direct attention; neither is inferred from simple scene presence.

### Privacy and creator choice are foundational

Attention measurement can become highly sensitive, especially if it relies on eye tracking, cameras, biometrics, or detailed behavioral telemetry. Do not assume such signals are available or may be collected. Measurement must be disclosed, purpose-limited, minimized, authorized by the host and applicable platform/OS rules, and independently optional where required. Provide a non-tracked experience path. Hidden sensor capture or covert behavioral profiling must not be a condition of play.

Advertising is not silently enabled by publication or by the presence of a creator venue. Creator consent, audience/host policy, advertiser license, content suitability, and measurement permission are separate decisions. This section is future research, not a current CreatorNetwork advertising feature or approved billing model.

## 7. Cross-domain ownership: do not turn CreatorNetwork into the whole Workshop

| Concern | Primary owner / authority | CreatorNetwork's relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Creator identity and disclosure | Profiles | Reference approved identity/persona projections; do not authenticate via display names. |
| Provenance and publication rights references | CreatorNetwork, coordinated with source/authoring domains | Preserve origin, derivation, source-work, license, and rights references in portable publication context. |
| Experience authoring/finalization | Experiences | Publish finalized Experience references; do not recreate editor or runtime-manifest contracts. |
| MicroBundle meaning and storage | MicroBundleDomain / MicroBundleRepository | Reference stable artifacts and provenance; do not duplicate bytes or repository semantics. |
| Ontology identity and classification | Ontology | Ensure creator works, source works, representations, venues, tools, and placements can be meaningfully represented; do not invent a parallel taxonomy. |
| Simulation and sculpting physics | Physics and specialized authoring tools | Reference authored capabilities/outputs; do not embed physics engines in publication contracts. |
| Device capture and semantic interaction | UserIO and host/platform adapters | Consume finalized, authorized outputs; do not acquire devices or grant OS permissions. |
| Runtime composition and execution | FSM_COS + trusted host | Provide content references/rights; host decides what may be resolved and executed. |
| Workflow sequencing | FSM_Foundry / current workflow owner | Offer narrow domain operations; do not build another generic scheduler. |
| Rendering and visualization | Renderer / Visualization / GUI and host | Publish representation intent and compatible references; do not prescribe a universal renderer. |
| Payments and royalty accounting | Future Economy/accounting/payment domain | Supply provenance/license facts; do not calculate or move money in the portable contract. |
| Advertising campaigns and billing | Future advertising/commerce domain plus host | Preserve creator/source rights and placement references; do not infer attention or bill advertisers. |

The Ontology is the Workshop's meaning system. A MicroBundle is a reusable composition/artifact form, not a substitute for provenance or rights. A publication can reference many works and representations while preserving their distinct ancestry and permissions.

## 8. Minimal future proof: museum source to independent Experience

Do not implement the entire vision at once. A first provenance/representation proof should be deliberately small and independently testable:

1. A museum creator publishes a venue/Experience reference and separately identified resources: building/layout, plaque text, original artwork, and an approved simplified artwork representation.
2. The original artwork's source-work identity, asserted originator, evidence state, and rights are distinct from the museum's role as venue publisher/host.
3. The museum explicitly permits reuse of layout/plaque resources and permits only the approved simplified representation of the restricted artwork for a stated action/audience.
4. An independent Experience consumer requests semantic roles rather than hard-coding museum storage URLs. The provider resolves each request to an authorized representation or returns a clear denial/incompatibility result.
5. The consumer records the exact resolved source and representation version in its own derivation/composition ancestry.
6. A later rights-policy change or takedown blocks future resolution and cache fill; controlled caches are invalidated and verified, while already exported copies are reported as outside guaranteed control.
7. A second museum can publish a different policy, including high-resolution reuse of selected works and exclusions for others, without changing the consumer's core composition logic.

Acceptance requires tests for partial authorization, source-controlled substitution, lineage preservation through swaps, policy changes/revocation, attribution propagation, provider failure, unknown rights state (fail closed for restricted uses), and independent consumer operation. Do not modify the current v1 wire DTO until compatibility and migration are explicitly designed.

## 9. Relationship to the rights-holder claim lifecycle

The external rights-holder portal and takedown lifecycle in [the domain review](DOMAIN_REVIEW.md#138-domain-decision-external-rights-holder-portal-disputes-and-package-takedown) complements this model:

- provenance identifies declared sources, derivatives, and affected descendants;
- rights assertions and licenses describe permitted use;
- claim review determines how a disputed assertion is handled;
- a recorded decision propagates to publication access, representation resolution, controlled caches, and future imports;
- licensing can resolve a dispute constructively where the claimant has authority to grant the requested rights.

A claim is not proof, and a provenance edge is not a license. Preserve due process, evidence, counter-notices, appeals, and jurisdiction-specific legal review.

## 10. Current implementation reality and explicit non-goals

At the reviewed development commit, the existing contract is much narrower:

- `ContentReference` has an opaque ID, media type, optional byte length, optional SHA-256, and optional source URI.
- `ChannelPublication` has creator identity, channel metadata, revision, content references, and one optional attribution string.
- `PublicationValidator` validates basic identifiers/metadata and digest shape; it does not validate provenance, source-work identity, rights, or licensing.
- `MediaAssetDescriptor` describes finalized bytes and integrity, not authorship or authorization.
- `FileMediaStore` streams bytes, computes SHA-256, and verifies optional expected length/digest.
- `ICreatorNetworkAccessPolicy` is a host enforcement seam for publication and asset read/write; it does not provide provenance or rights policy.
- The current node is loopback-development-only. There is no production identity, public rights-holder portal, representation resolver, provenance graph, royalty ledger, or advertising measurement/billing system.

Do not retrofit all of these concepts into `ChannelPublication` v1. First finish the CreatorNetwork domain gate, identify the smallest portable provenance/rights vocabulary, and prove it with an independent publisher and consumer. Keep implementation, legal review, and monetization behind their own gates.
